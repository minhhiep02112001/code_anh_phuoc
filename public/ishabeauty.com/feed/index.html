Hyperpigmentation can refer to any darkening of the skin. Thankfully there is help to be had for the condition, but before we dive into products and treatments for hyperpigmentation, let’s dig a little deeper into the topic.

Some of us, including myself, are prone to uneven pigmentation because of higher melanin levels. On the other hand, there are women who have been pregnant and have gone through menopause and have never had pigmentation problems.

If you are like me, you need to ask yourself; when did you first notice it? Was it during pregnancy? Exacerbated by excessive heat? What triggered it? If you know the root of the cause, hyperpigmentation can be more effectively treated.

Types of Hyperpigmentation

Melasma

Melasma, commonly called pregnancy mask or chloasma, is a skin condition affecting over 6 million women in the United States. Melasma can develop most anywhere on the body, but is most commonly seen around the perimeters of the face. Melasma is caused by an overproduction of pigment called melanin, which is produced by melanocyte cells. Melanocyte cells are found in the dermis, which is the deepest layer of the skin. Melanocytes produce melanin, bundle with melanosomes and begin their move to the surface of the skin and disperse causing hyperpigmentation. 

The cause of melasma is not fully understood, however factors that come into play are pregnancy hormones, menopause, UV exposure and genetics. Men and women with deeper skin tones are more prone to melasma. Melasma can be managed, however, one can not fully get rid of it.

Sun Spots & Age Spots

In middle age, many women, especially fair-skinned women, report seeing sun spots, age spots, and “liver spots” crop up. They present as brown spots and range in size from 0.2 to 2 cm. A common misconception about these spots is that they are caused only by UV exposure. But did you know that the advice to stay out of the sun and apply SPF is not a sure proof way to prevent them?

Studies have shown that the heat is also a contributing factor because it stimulates melanin activity which in turn results in discoloration and the formation of brown spots. This means that no matter how much sunscreen you apply, you can still get sunspots. So, while UV sources like the sun are among the top triggers, there are other triggers.

To prevent sunspots from developing, avoid being in the direct sunlight between 10am and 4pm. These are the hottest hours of the day and UV rays are at their peak. Since sunspots can be heat activated, keep the temperature of your skin cool. Sources of heat can be a BBQ grill, a hairdryer, even the temperature in a hot yoga class! If you know that you will be in the heat, keep a cool towel nearby and mist your face with cool water. This is not an excuse to not wear sunscreen—you still need to prevent burning.

Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (PIH)

Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH) refers to the dark, pigmented marks that sometimes form after a trauma is caused to the skin. PIH is often the result of acne, burns, friction or aggressive clinical treatments such as chemical peels, dermabrasion, or lasers.  This type of pigmentation can improve with time and is treatable with topical products including acids. 

Freckles 

Like melasma, freckles are caused by melanin. When sunlight hits the skin, the skin goes into a survival mode and starts producing more melanin to protect all the skin’s layers. If you have melanin building in one place it will result in freckles. Freckles may appear darker as we age.

]]> https://ishabeauty.com/healing-power/feed/ 0 10 Essential Skincare Tips for Radiant Skin https://ishabeauty.com/skincare-tips/ https://ishabeauty.com/skincare-tips/#respond Sun, 12 Dec 2021 17:36:00 +0000 https://ishabeauty.com/?p=1848 Achieving radiant, glowing skin is a goal many of us strive for, but it can feel overwhelming with so many skincare products and tips out there. The good news is that a consistent skincare routine with simple steps can make a world of difference. Here are 10 essential skincare tips to help you get the glowing, healthy skin you’ve always wanted.

1. Cleansing is Key

Proper cleansing is the foundation of any skincare routine. Your skin is constantly exposed to dirt, pollution, sweat, and oils that can clog your pores and lead to breakouts. Make sure you use a gentle cleanser that suits your skin type—whether that’s a cream-based cleanser for dry skin or a foaming one for oily skin. Cleansing twice a day—morning and night—helps remove impurities and prepares your skin for the next steps.

2. Exfoliate Regularly

Exfoliation helps remove dead skin cells that can make your skin look dull and prevent your skincare products from absorbing effectively. However, avoid over-exfoliating, which can irritate your skin. Depending on your skin type, exfoliate 1-3 times a week using a gentle scrub or a chemical exfoliant with ingredients like AHAs or BHAs for smoother, more radiant skin.

3. Hydrate, Hydrate, Hydrate

Hydration is essential for glowing skin. Drinking plenty of water throughout the day is one of the best things you can do for your skin. Additionally, use a moisturizer suited for your skin type—lightweight gel formulas work well for oily skin, while richer creams are perfect for dry or mature skin. Hydrated skin looks plump, smooth, and radiant, so never skip this step.

4. Use Sunscreen Every Day

UV rays are one of the biggest contributors to premature aging, wrinkles, and dark spots. Protect your skin by applying sunscreen with at least SPF 30 every morning, regardless of the weather. This helps prevent sun damage and preserves your skin’s youthful glow. Don’t forget to reapply if you’re outdoors for extended periods.

5. Add Antioxidants to Your Routine

Antioxidants like vitamin C, E, and green tea are fantastic for brightening the skin and protecting it from environmental stressors like pollution. Vitamin C, in particular, helps to even out skin tone, reduce pigmentation, and give you a radiant glow. Look for serums or creams that are rich in antioxidants to boost your skin’s health.

6. Get Enough Sleep

Sleep is when your skin regenerates and repairs itself, so make sure you’re getting 7-9 hours of quality rest every night. Lack of sleep can lead to dark circles, dullness, and an overall tired appearance. To enhance skin repair, consider using a rich night cream or an overnight mask to lock in moisture and nutrients while you sleep.

7. Don’t Skip Your Eye Cream

The delicate skin around your eyes is often the first to show signs of aging and fatigue. Using an eye cream with ingredients like caffeine, hyaluronic acid, or peptides can help reduce puffiness, dark circles, and fine lines. Apply the product gently with your ring finger, as this helps prevent unnecessary tugging on the skin.

8. Watch Your Diet

What you eat plays a significant role in the health of your skin. A balanced diet rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and healthy fats can help keep your skin radiant. Omega-3 fatty acids found in foods like salmon, walnuts, and avocados can boost skin hydration. Antioxidant-rich foods like berries, spinach, and sweet potatoes can also fight oxidative stress and protect the skin from aging.

9. Be Gentle with Your Skin

It can be tempting to try various treatments and products to achieve glowing skin, but overdoing it can lead to irritation. Avoid harsh scrubbing or using too many active ingredients at once. Stick to a consistent routine with products that suit your skin type, and always patch-test new products to avoid adverse reactions.

10. Practice Stress Management

Chronic stress can wreak havoc on your skin, causing breakouts, inflammation, and dullness. Practice stress-relieving activities like yoga, meditation, deep breathing, or even regular exercise to improve your skin’s health. A calmer mind often leads to healthier, glowing skin.

Final Thoughts

Radiant skin doesn’t happen overnight, but with a consistent skincare routine that focuses on cleansing, hydration, protection, and nourishment, you can achieve a healthy, glowing complexion. Remember, taking care of your skin is a marathon, not a sprint. By following these 10 essential tips and being patient, you’ll notice visible improvements in your skin’s texture, tone, and overall radiance. Start today, and enjoy the confidence that comes with glowing skin!

Retinoids vs. Retinol

The two most common topical vitamin A products on the market are retinoids and retinol. Let’s dive into what they are and the difference between the two.

Retinoid, or tretinoin, is a synthetic vitamin A derivative. It is available as a topical cream, gel or liquid, and is used to treat acne, sun-damaged skin and signs of aging. It can’t erase deep wrinkles, but it can help improve the appearance of surface wrinkles, fine lines and dark spots. It may sound counterintuitive, but tretinoin works by irritating the skin, speeding up the life cycle and turnover of skin cells, resulting in a more youthful appearance. Brand name retinoids include Retin A, Retin-A Micro, and Renova. Other retinoids include Tazarotene (brand name Tazorac) and Adapalene (brand name Differin and now sold without a prescription). Because of their potency and strength, most retinoids require a prescription from your doctor.

Retinol is a natural vitamin A derivative found in many over-the-counter skin care products and does not require a prescription. When retinol is applied to skin, it slowly and gently converts to retinoic acid. Retinol products are considered milder and less potent than prescription tretinoin for its time-release factor. This means it’s delivered into the skin slowly, over a period of hours, instead of all at once upon application (as retinoids are). This is why retinol users often experience less, if any, irritation. Retinol is ideal for sensitive, reactive skin types and those with moderate sun damage. Make no mistake, despite being gentler, retinol is still extremely effective and can deliver the same results as a prescription retinoid with long-term use.

Which is Right for You?

If you have skin challenges that need tackling such as severe acne, talk to your dermatologist about a prescription retinoid. If you would rather ease into the use of topical vitamin A, give retinol a try. It is the gentler way to go and does not require a prescription.

How to Dermaroll

  1. Sanitize the dermaroller. Always, always sanitize your dermaroller before and after use. If your device does not include a sanitizer, submerging the roller head in a small container of rubbing alcohol for 3-5 minutes will do the job. 
  2. Prep your skin. Cleanse and tone your skin.
  3. Apply your favorite serum. For dryness, consider a serum with hyaluronic acid. For hyperpigmentation, a Vitamin C-based serum would be a good choice. Apply a light layer to the area(s) being treated, with the exception of eyelids. You do not need to use any product in conjunction with dermarolling, but doing so has so many advantages.
  4. Start rolling. Technique is important. Divide your face into four sections. Gently roll over the skin with your derma roller in a vertical, horizontal, and then diagonal direction three to four times per area. I recommend doing this twice which should take about 5 minutes. Take extra care when rolling under your eyes and around the lips as these areas are more sensitive.  Apply just enough pressure that is comfortably tolerated. You may notice some mild redness and tingling. That’s a good indicator that you are rolling right! Please note: Never dermaroll inflamed skin or acne.
  5. Apply another layer of serum. Finish your rolling with second application of your favorite serum.
  6. Sanitize again. Clean your roller thoroughly following the instructions in Step 1. If left uncleaned, harmful bacteria can grow on the head and needles of the roller, which can lead to breakouts.

How Often Should I Dermaroll?

If you are just starting out, roll 1-2 times per week. Gradually work your way up to 4-5 times a week. I recommend dermarolling at night so that any redness you experience can dissipate overnight.

How long does permanent makeup last?

The beauty industry calls it permanent makeup, but permanent makeup is actually semi-permanent and lasts, on average, three to five years. Depending on where the pigment is placed, some makeup may fade sooner. For example, permanent pigment placed on the eyeline or the lash line may last one to three years, while pigment placed directly on the skin may take five years to fade. Fading can be attributed to factors such as your skin type, your age, how well your skin reacts to pigment, the amount of sun exposure you have, and the types of products that you use on your skin. Some of these are factors that you have no control over, while others are general lifestyle choices that can shorten or extend results.

Do permanent makeup applications hurt?

Permanent makeup is sometimes referred to as tattooed makeup because the process is similar to tattooing. In the case of permanent makeup, a needle is used to deposit ink into the first layers of the skin ONLY. With a body tattoo the needle goes much deeper. Your tolerance for pain certainly can come into play, but the application of a numbing cream prior to permanent makeup procedures typically eliminates most, if not all, discomfort.
